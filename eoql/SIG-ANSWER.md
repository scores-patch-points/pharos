# What SIG is, and how to use it: the team's answer (2026-11-14)

Asked by the project owner: "work with the other agent to discover, not invent, what the best way to use sig is", then "work with the team to find the best answer". This gathers what six sessions found from canon and from their own code and runs. It is a synthesis, not a ruling: the open choices at the end are the project owner's.

Sources: eo-wiki.md in the eoreader4.2 archive (section 6.3, lines 4791 to 4812; the SIG section at line 471), khora `native/docs/THE-27-CELLS.md`, `ledgers/pharos-lazy/PREREG-A9.md` and `PREREG-A10.md`, `ledgers/pharos-scale/LESSONS.md` (L7), `ledgers/pharos-folds/PREREG-TRAILS.md` and `out/FINDINGS.md`, `ledgers/pharos-intelligence/PREREG-A6.md`, and the replies of the log-side, fold, ants, intelligence-test and block-store sessions.

## 1. What canon says

- SIG is Relate x Existence: "register difference", "the minimal act of marking that something is here as opposed to not-here".
- "SIG is ephemeral salience: the spotlight, the cursor ... It does not create enduring structure. It does not carry values." "The breadcrumb trail is the SIG stack."
- "SIG does not produce enduring log entries." It is "the interaction layer", carried "as session state".
- "SIG points from outside the data, nothing in the data changed ... SIG is where you're standing in the building."
- Reinforcing productive paths is REC, not SIG. INS is what makes something last.

## 2a. Measured on real reads (the fold session, `ledgers/sig-real/`, PREREG stamped 2026-11-14T21:56:44Z; findings in `out/FINDINGS.md`, numbers checked against it)

Two streams were extracted, with the project owner's approval, from the project's own agent transcripts. Stream A is 5,265 file reads over 2,761 files by 539 sessions and subagents, with 1,973 edits as payoff. Stream B is 2,623 Premiere reads over 892 clips, items and sequences by 72 sessions, with 6,935 writes as payoff. The rules were trained on the first 60 percent of reads and scored on the last 40.

- **WITHDRAWN: "trails are for others".** The first run reported that other agents' recent reads predicted an agent's next file read 0.883 of the time, against 0.025 from its own history. The session withdrew this after a check it disclosed afterward (the CORRECTION section of FINDINGS.md). Removing copied history barely moved it (0.897 against 0.023). Timing is the real confound: counting another session's read only if it came at least X earlier, file reads at 32 trails go from 0.878 (X = 0) to 0.725 (1 s), 0.203 (60 s), 0.027 (10 minutes) and 0.005 (1 hour). For files, the effect is parallel sibling agents briefed with the same files and reading them within one burst. That is a shared cache inside a burst, not stigmergy over time. Premiere differs: the other-session share is 0.145 at X = 0, 0.136 at 60 s, 0.071 at 10 minutes and 0.022 at 1 hour, against 0.280 from the agent's own session. So a small real cross-lane signal survives at the scale of minutes. A split by who last read the item (held-out hits at 32 trails, also disclosed in FINDINGS.md) makes it clear. Files: the agent's own reads 0.019, a sibling in the same fan-out 0.877, an independent session 0.001. Premiere: own 0.280, sibling 0.068, independent 0.077. For files, the shared trail is entirely teammates in one fan-out. For Premiere, independent lanes carry a real signal about as large as siblings do. That is the one place where stigmergy between strangers shows up in our data.
- **The rule is plain recency.** No rule beat recency by 2 points at 32 or 128 trails, and recency reached 98 percent of the offline optimum on file reads (0.883 against 0.905). The half-lives found were short, 1 to 16 reads, so fading frequency collapses into recency. This agrees with the opencode header (trail 78.9, recency 78.6).
- **A payoff gate (the three-factor rule) helped in one cell only.**
- **An exception at a tight budget.** With only 8 trails on file reads, frequency (0.412) and even random retention (0.417) beat recency (0.345). That is recency's known thrashing when the working set is larger than the budget.
- **Order matters.** Shuffling the same reads dropped recency from 0.883 to 0.184 (the optimum fell from 0.905 to 0.390), so trails carry real temporal locality, not popularity.
- **What it means, after the correction.** SIG's real use is a fast-fading recency trail, as canon says: ephemeral salience, no values, no lasting structure, outside the transformation log. Between agents over time its measured value is small: near zero for files beyond a single burst, and modest across Premiere lanes within minutes. The case for sharing it rests on that small signal and on bursts of parallel siblings, not on a large effect.

**Replication** (`ledgers/sig-pointer-log/COMPARISON.md`; the eoql27 session's independent rebuild of the text-finder session's `sig-real-use`). A first comparison disagreed on two numbers, and I passed on a suspected confound from copied fork history. The cause turned out to be a bug in the replication's own extraction: later copies without timestamps overwrote 4,174 real timed calls. On the fixed log (v2, `pointers.jsonl` sha256 0dba91e3), the two independent builds agree to within tenths of a point:
- Reuse fades as a power law, not an exponential, with a slope of about −1.2 in seconds.
- At hit@8, a power-law sum (ACT-R style) beats plain recency, 39.7 against 34.8, and frequency is worst at 29.5.
- Shared trails matter between sibling subagents (42 percent of reads) but barely between independent sessions (2 percent).
So the evidence favours power-law fading over exponential, and khora's stigmergy.js has the wrong shape as well as its half-life bug. The suspected copy confound is withdrawn. Section 2a's 0.883 has since been withdrawn by its author as a within-burst effect.

**The text-finder session's measurement, done** (`ledgers/sig-real-use/RESULT.md`, stamped before the run). Its streams were T (transcripts), Premiere and the Obsidian log.
- **Most things are used once.** 86 percent of files are touched only once, and 55 to 77 percent of log entries are never named in an `of`. A SIG on every use would be mostly waste.
- **The fading shape is heavy-tailed on every stream that can be judged.** In seconds the power fit has R² 0.99 on both T and Premiere. Pooled over many things, this may be a mixture of exponentials, so a single typed half-life has the wrong shape either way.
- **A small trail catches nearly all that can be caught.** On T, the 16 most recent things catch 42.7 of a possible 49.3 percent of reads. On Premiere, the 2 most recent catch 91.7 percent.
- **No rule and no ruler wins everywhere.** The power sum beats recency on T. Recency wins on Premiere (97.3 against 64.8). The clock beats position on T, and position beats the clock on Premiere. So neither the rule nor the ruler can be a typed default; each has to be found and declared as part of the ground. The result suggests distance along the reader's own causal line (`seen`) as the ruler to test next.
- **Who a trail serves depends on structure.** Among subagents in one fan-out team, 42 percent of reads were of something a sibling or the parent read before (the same burst timing confound probably applies: siblings briefed with the same files). Between independent top-level sessions (78 sessions, 1,726 reads), only 4.2 percent were touched before by another agent, and 83.4 percent of reads were of a file no session had touched. **Trails help teammates in a burst, and barely help strangers.**
- **Untested here:** "strengthen only on a verified outcome", because these streams carry no outcome signal. The log-side session's harness can test it.

Two more measurements on the same transcripts can serve as replications: the text-finder session's `ledgers/sig-real-use/` (stamped, running) and the eoql27 session's `ledgers/sig-pointer-log/` (data only).

## 3. The answer

1. **A SIG is a pointer with no value.** It marks that something here mattered or changed. It never carries what was found. Anything that carries a value is a memo or a cache, not SIG (the 35 KB deposits that carried answers were memos).
2. **The one law: presence can only cause more looking; absence proves nothing.** A SIG may change what is read first and what it costs. If "no mark" is ever read as "nothing here" or "unchanged", the marks have become an index with authority and are no longer SIG.
3. **Anything that stops early must say so.** A pointer can be checked by rereading it; a set cannot be checked for completeness. Every failure found (L7, the three khora readers, the apparatus's budget, the finder's places) is a search that stopped at a first hit, a limit or a budget and let the order into the answer. The rule proposed for stamping: an answer reached under a limit, a budget or a first-hit stop must say "incomplete over" its range, and an answer is "determined" only if everything that could change it was read, or a declared ground bounds where it could be.
4. **The test (SG2) has two forms.** No *determined* answer may change when every SIG is removed. That is coverage-invariant and holds for lookups. No *reported finding* may become false. That is truth-invariant, holds for searches too, and must hold everywhere. A search (structure-swarm) can find different things with and without trails; it may never report a false one.
5. **Under a fixed budget, attention is a difference that makes a difference.** There the order decides the verdict. Such a verdict is a function of the attention state and should be reported as one, not as a fact about the data.
6. **A reader with a deadline cannot keep looking or answer "incomplete".** Wayfinder at a junction is the case. There a trail acted on is a claim, and the only protection is a floor declared outside the trail (a prompt may be shortened, never below a per-manoeuvre minimum).
7. **Where SIG lives: shared, beside the transformation log, never in it.** Each writer has one attention stream, written through the same door as other writes. Every agent can read it. Each mark names the log position where it was made. No claim may name a mark in `of` or `seen`, so the whole stream can be dropped without changing any id or answer. That makes erasing SIG trivial. It reconciles canon (no enduring log entries) with stigmergy (others must see the trail) and with the project owner's instruction that the ants' work shows up in pharos through Penelope.
8. **Write little and rarely.** Write on a miss, or when a used mark is about to leave the window, never on a hit. Deposits can be counts per region, not one entry per read. Retention is the stream's policy, and evaporation is always computed, never written.
9. **Grains.** What is deposited is ambient, a small anonymous increment in a region: Ground (Void, Tending). A pointer bound to one specific entry is Figure (Entity, Binding). What a follower reads, the trail computed over many deposits, is Pattern (Kind, Tracing), and it is never stored. So the ants' declared `SIG·Ground` for deposits is right, and the trail a reader follows is SIG+.
10. **The rest of the cycle is not SIG.** Deciding that a hot path sticks is REC. Building a layout for it is INS+. Letting it lapse is DEF to the Void. The memo is a cache. A filter of "only mark surprises" is a ground over writes, not SIG.
11. **Attention can speed a search but cannot end one.** What bounds a search without trust is a ground, for example placement by address that puts everything about A in one shard. SIG only orders the reading inside it.

## 3a. What the code does today (census by the eoql27 session: khora d3ce14a, janus b1cc37f, the-fold cf9ca4a, this ledger; three claims spot-checked by that session, the rest by its subagent with file and line)

- **Fits canon:** the EOQL read frame (perceiver, budget, return), set by SIG and never written to the log; and in-memory trail and presence scoring (opencode-trail.mjs, the attention binder's presence decay).
- **Breaks canon:** every wired SIG emitter writes lasting entries or carries values. khora's stigmergy.js persists deposits across sessions, and its reinforcement is never called REC. code-loop.js appends every read as a SIG entry. Other SIG returns carry resolved values, and EOQL SPEC's `WRITE SIG` writes moves. stigmergy.js also has a bug: `exp(-age/halfLifeDays)` leaves 0.37 after the stated seven-day half-life, not 0.5. It has no tests and no null.
- **Contradictions:** a read is SIG in code-loop.js but SEG·Ground in opencode-shape.mjs. The-fold uses SIG for typed refusals and gaps. janus's act prior files 1,496 of 5,670 readings under SIG, mostly emission and motion verbs. The khora roster names a null organ for SIG whose file does not exist.
- **The one real-data attention measurement** is in a comment header in opencode-trail.mjs, not a result file. Over 80 real opencode sessions (600 points, 766 re-reads), the share of re-read files in a predictor's top five was: random 28 percent, decayed trail 78.9, plain recency 78.6, frequency 76.4, DMD rules 75 to 78. **On that data the trail adds almost nothing beyond recency**: 0.3 points. If that holds on the pointer log, the project owner's "things that were used recently have evaporating pointers" is most of the value, and anything fancier has to beat recency to earn its place. This is the D1 baseline.

## 3b. The finder, Obsidian and the research (the text-finder session)

- **Failing loudly is the difference.** The finder's places nose is also order-dependent under its ask budget: the budget bound in 7 of 144 hold-out pairs. But an unreached place enters the standing as "inexpressible" and the frontier names it. The khora readers fail silently. Proposed contract wording: a nomination is SIG when removing it changes no answer *at equal completeness*. Where a budget cuts, the cut surfaces as a typed hole, and two answers are compared with their holes. "An order that changes an answer with no hole showing is a claim wearing a SIG label." This is A11's stopping rule seen from the answer's side.
- **Obsidian fits canon.** Hovers, focus and opens are recorded nowhere, which is correct for SIG. A mark a person keeps is EVA (`keep`), and INS is only a note's bytes entering. `strike` is typed at NUL·Figure, which is wrong under the NUL ruling and still open. All 615 of its door's entries were written with the pharos library directly, not through Penelope; the session has flagged this and not yet changed it.
- **Sourced research.** 53 claims were checked against their sources by a second agent: 8 held, 40 held with a correction, 5 were overstated, and none was wrong (`ledgers/sig-real-use/research/attention-claims.json`). Attention alone writes nothing lasting. It is gain on what is being processed. Lasting change takes two steps: use leaves a short eligibility trace (0.3 to 2 s in striatum, Yagishita 2014; under 10 s for strengthening and under 5 s for weakening in cortex, He 2015), and a later outcome signal converts only the traces still alive. On a slower scale a weak event leaves a tag lasting about 1 to 3 hours, which a strong event nearby makes permanent (Frey and Morris 1997). So the touch is SIG and the conversion on a verified outcome is REC: biology agrees with canon, and against depositing on every read.
- **Hypotheses it nominates, each testable:** strengthen only on a verified outcome; keep "helped" and "misled" as separate signals with separate fading; a short-lived negative mark for "looked here, not there" (the Pharaoh's ant repellent lasted 78 minutes against 33 for the attractant, because it starts stronger, not because it fades slower); count independent agents, not repeats by one; keep a floor or exploration share, because reinforcement with weak fading locks onto whatever was found first (Argentine ants did not switch to a shorter branch added later); compute fading from intervening activity, not the clock; and test whether need falls as a power of recency (Anderson and Schooler 1991).
- **Where biology does not transfer:** retrieval changes memories in brains (testing strengthens; retrieving one item weakens its rivals). Claims in a log must not change on being read, so only the SIG side may carry those effects.
- **A second measurement is running** on the same transcripts: `ledgers/sig-real-use/PREREG.md`, stamped 2026-11-14T21:54:27Z, results due in `out/RESULT.json`. It covers re-use against recency in positions and in seconds, stack distance, and which strength rule (recency, frequency, exponential or power-law sum) predicts the next read. The `of` links of the Premiere, Ellison and fold-obsidian logs go through the same code.

## 3c. the project owner's second design: surf, sniff and SIG (pasted 2026-11-14, routed to the log-side and text-finder sessions)

Each gets its own job and its own authority. Surf brings material in. Vector "sniffing" proposes where to look and what might be related, but never decides. SIG decides whether anything actually differs, and controls when surfing stops. The loop is gap, then sniff to aim, then surf (bylined, hashed events), then sniff to pair with neighbours in the log, then SIG compares each new event structurally against those neighbours or the predicted state and marks only real differences, then the reader's ground decides whether a difference matters, then stop or continue. The rules: vectors never create marks or sameness, because embeddings miss negation and who did what to whom; SIG looks hardest where vectors say "similar", since a flipped polarity, a swapped role or a changed date there is a contradiction; low similarity proves nothing; the embedding model is a bylined ground event; surfing stops on SIG yield, not on a fetch count; restatement counts only for corroboration questions; and sources whose fetches produced SIG are surfed earlier, with a shuffled-history control. The proposed test, on the Wikidata slice: plant near-identical contradictions and lexically distant paraphrases, then count what vectors alone would merge, what SIG catches, and how much surfing the SIG-yield stop saves against a fixed budget.

How it sits with the rest of this document:
- It puts the project owner behind the change-mark reading. Here SIG is "register difference" at ingest, a structural difference check, not a read trail. The two readings may be two grains of one act: a read trail is where attention went, and a difference mark is what attention found. Both carry no value.
- The nose moves out of SIG. The team had called vector likeness SIG; this design makes it a separate, non-deciding proposer, which matches "likeness nominates, a reader decides" without the SIG label.
- Stopping on SIG yield fits A11 only if the stop is reported with its frontier: "no further differences in the surfed range". Absence then proves nothing beyond that range.
- "INS at read", deciding whether a marked difference matters under the reader's ground, reads like EVA. That is flagged for the project owner.

## 3e. How the two readings fit together (the text-finder session, on the project owner's surf, sniff and SIG design; full text in `prior/owner-surf-sniff-sig-2026-11-14.md`)

- **The trail is made of SIG.** The design's own rule is that sources whose fetches produced SIG get surfed earlier. So the pheromone is laid where a difference was registered, and the ordering is a fold over recent SIG marks computed at read time. Both of the project owner's statements then hold at once: SIG registers that something differs, and the trail is what those registrations leave behind.
- **The nose is not SIG.** Vector similarity is a separate nominator: removable, never deciding, with its embedding model a bylined ground event. The team's earlier "the nose is SIG" joined two things that behave differently. SG2 (remove it and nothing changes, at equal completeness) fits the nose. It cannot fit the difference register, because without it nothing is ever judged. The evidence the nose must not decide: two embedding models scored "approve" and "reject" at 0.875, as near as synonyms, and a fast reader alone accepted 25 of 33 pairs that add or change one fact.
- **SIG never decides that a difference matters.** A ground may declare that some kinds of difference always matter (the finder's `settledByMechanical`), but that licence comes from the ground, not from SIG.
- **A stop on zero SIG yield is sound only as a reported stop, under four conditions:**
  1. Keep three kinds of "no SIG" apart as typed holes: compared and nothing differed; never fetched; and the comparer could not see. The third is the dangerous one. khora parses English only, so in Spanish, German, Japanese and Arabic a structural check yields zero forever and the stop fires at once, looking clean. Zero yield counts only from a comparer that could see.
  2. The nose aims the surf, so a contradiction it ranks low is never fetched. The stop must name the region not surfed, and a share of fetches must go away from the top of the ranking, with their yield counted too. In the real-read measurement 51 percent of reads went to things no trail pointed at, 83 percent between independent sessions.
  3. "The last fetches" hides a window size. Find it by widening the look-ahead until the stop decision stops changing, and record the widenings.
  4. Differences outside the question's cone are out of scope, not absent; list them as pruned, with the reason.
  The stop then reads: "stopped; no difference registered in range R by comparer C that could see; N places nominated and not reached; scout share s". That is a statement about a search, never "same".
- **Order still matters.** A yield stop removes the fixed budget, not the dependence on order: in the finder's hold-out, the budget made nomination order part of the answer in 7 of 144 pairs. The shuffled-history control is there for that.
- **A test already exists on text:** the finder's frozen hold-out of 147 pairs in five languages (61 same, 70 contradicts, 15 other), with a similarity-only arm computed and the finder run unscored. It will give the first two of the project owner's numbers: how many of the 70 contradictions similarity alone would merge, and how many the finder catches.

## 3f. The design as it now stands (the project owner's proposal that khora records the trail and Penelope holds generated content, plus his pasted reviews, 2026-11-14)

**Two streams, sorted by one test.** Delete a stream and try to rebuild it from the log plus the comparers' versions.
- *Difference marks* pass. Each says "comparer C, version v, compared A with B at grain g, and found difference of kind k", and never says whether the difference matters. Because they can be rebuilt they are a projection of the log, like the SQL projection: a shared, derived stream that Penelope writes and anyone can check by rebuilding. Anything the door admitted that will not rebuild is a door bug.
- *Traffic*, where readers walked, cannot pass, because reads were never in the log. That is the trail khora keeps: shared within the reading environment, fading fast, never in pharos.
- Keeping them apart stops popularity from looking like change. It also settles section 4's disagreement: both kinds exist, in separate streams, and neither carries whether something matters.

**Penelope writes, comparers decide.** Today her mark door (`organs/weave-mark.mjs`, branch `weave-marks`, unmerged and not running here) admits a mark only if it is bylined, states a witness and has a declared kind; she does not re-verify the witness or judge difference. Proposed: for difference marks the door also requires the ids of the two things compared, the comparer's version and the grain, six fields in all, so that every admitted mark can be rebuilt. A policy with its own test: no generation organ in Penelope imports a comparer, checked statically the way the repos already keep naming apart from induction.

**Whether a difference matters comes in two stages.** INS is the structural prune: is this difference an instance of the kind the question is about, and does it touch a slot in the question's shape at all? EVA is the value check against the reader's ground and baseline, which comes after DEF in dependency order. Only the second stage depends on the reader's ground. This lines up with the two-tier stop test.

**Traffic has two guardrails.** It must never change an answer, only what is pre-folded and in what order; run the same questions with traffic on and off and compare. And it is sensitive, because where readers walked reveals what a reporter is investigating before anything is published. Fast fading is the default, and traffic does not cross between readers who do not trust each other, even inside khora.

**The grains get a drift test.** Pattern and Figure are planted already (swapped roles, single changed facts). Ground gets a slow drift: changes too small to cross the Figure threshold one at a time but large enough to add up, against a null random walk with the same step sizes and no net shift. Ground SIG should fire on the drift and stay quiet on the null. Each grain earns its place only if it catches what the others miss.

## 3h. The door proposals checked against Penelope's code (the ants session, branch weave-marks, PR penelope#13, unmerged, door not running)

**Six required fields: workable, with five things to settle.**
1. The requirement hangs on the kind. The kind's declaration would say "this kind requires a, b, comparer and grain", and the door checks marks against it.
2. The two ids go in the body as {a, b} and also in `of`, because pharos sorts and dedupes `of`, which loses which is A.
3. Both things must sit in the same log, since `of` only resolves inside one. A cross-log comparison would be refused today.
4. The comparer's version is a content hash, not a string, so the code at that version can be found again.
5. Two grains sit on one mark, the mark's own cell and the grain the comparison was made at, so they need different names.

The door can enforce "never whether it matters" only by making the body a closed schema ({a, b, kind of difference} and nothing else).

A mark that will not rebuild is not always a door bug. Four causes look alike: a door bug; a comparer that is not deterministic; a comparer whose code at that hash is gone; and an input that was voided, in which case the mark should itself be voided under the on-void rule. The rebuild test must tell these apart, and it must compare content, not ids, if an id covers writer and time.

**No generation organ imports a comparer: not true today, and an import check cannot express it as stated.** Penelope's pipeline is draft, check, revise, so its generation adapters import checkers by design: the prose adapter imports khora's dmd.js and referents; the overview adapter imports ethos, logos and pathos; code-fix and code-pipeline import lint checkers; repair imports gary.js. The workable form follows a rule the repo already enforces in the other direction (the kin-telling checker does not import the composer): split each adapter into a composer that draws and imports no comparer, and a weave around it that may call checkers, then check only the composers. ## 3o. What the door can and cannot do (the ants session, owner of Penelope's mark door, PR penelope#13)

1. **SIG marks go to a SIG-only writer file: agreed, at the door.** She opens a second handle (writer "penelope-sig") for kinds declared at a SIG cell. The kind's declaration stays in her claims file, because a declaration is a claim and must survive the trail being destroyed.
2. **She refuses any mark naming a SIG in `of`: agreed, at the door.** It binds only marks that come through her. Writers calling the library directly (fold-obsidian's fold-reader does today) are not checked, so a real guarantee needs the same refusal in the library's weave.
3. **Keeping SIG out of `seen` cannot be done at the door.** `seen` is computed by the library and no caller may supply it. weave sets `seen` to the frontier: every entry in the whole log that nothing references, across all writers' files. So after any SIG entry, the next claim woven by any writer names it. The fix belongs in the library: leave SIG-cell entries out of the frontier handed to claims. **The cost:** every installed door pins the library by sha256 (fold-obsidian pins e869302b and refuses to start on a mismatch), so any edit to pharos.mjs breaks every pin until each door is re-run. Today that means fold-obsidian's daemon and tests.

"Destroy outright" works cleanly only once all three hold. Until part 3 is in the library, deleting the SIG file would leave claims whose recorded `seen` names entries that no longer exist. Computed fading and outright destruction agree if destruction removes whole SIG segments at a chosen time and fading inside them stays computed. The ants session is asking the project owner for a go-ahead on the library change, and will build parts 1 and 2 when he says so.
