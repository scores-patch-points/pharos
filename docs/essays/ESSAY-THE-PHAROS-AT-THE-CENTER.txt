THE LIGHT THAT IS ALSO A CLOTH
==============================

On a shared log that keeps traces and gives no orders, what the ants, the weavers and the ship's navigators already paid for the idea, and what the best place to leave marks would have to refuse.

Standing: nomination, wide on purpose. The project is a film being cut in Adobe Premiere by several AI sessions at once, and the pharos is the shared append-only log they write to. This essay follows one idea about it as far as it will go: that the pharos is a stigmergic hub, a medium where agents coordinate through traces left in it and never through messages to one another, and (Proposal) that the best version would steer as little as it can: no scheduler, no orders, no addressed messages, though trails and no-entry marks would still bias where readers look first. This project has no measure of "best"; the only candidates are the stamped tests named in section 4, and none has run on real traffic. The essay goes wide, into insects, ships, looms, myth and file systems, and one kind of passage is strange on purpose. Five kinds of claim appear, and every claim carries one tag. A tag stands at the start or the end of the run of sentences it governs, and no run crosses a paragraph break.

- Result. Measured in this project, with the conditions stated. Nearly all of it ran once, on one shared Mac under heavy load.
- Built. Code exists and its tests pass, with the folder named.
- Prior art. From other fields. "Checked" means a source or citation record was opened, often only an abstract or secondary summary, which the text says. The rest is as I recall.
- Proposal. Stated, not tested.
- Play. Strange on purpose. A direction to look in, not a claim.

Three words of project vocabulary recur. Pre-registered means a test and its pass bar were written down before the run. Stamped means a pre-registered test frozen in a dated file, where any later change is a numbered amendment; "stamped, not built" means such an amendment exists and no code does. W-numbers name the stage-1 test rows in the lazy store's results file, and A-numbers name the amendments PREREG-A1.md to PREREG-A8.md. Section 7 also carries two labels that are not claims: Undecided, for calls that belong to the user, and Not checked.

Where this prose and the records disagree, the records win: ledgers/pharos-lazy/out/results.json, RULINGS-STAGE1.md and the amendments PREREG-A1.md to PREREG-A8.md beside it, ledgers/pharos-blocks/out/results.json, ledgers/pharos-sqlite/out/, ledgers/gateway-capture/out/, ledgers/pharos-intelligence/HARNESS-CHANGES.md, ledgers/DECISIONS-2026-11-13.md, and phasepost-labels/pharos-premiere/out/. One rule runs under all of it: the 27 cells and nine operators of experiential ontology (EO) are earned from data, never typed. Nothing below assigns a cell or an operator to a part of stigmergy. Where the question comes up, it is an open question tagged Play. Section 8, added on 2026-11-14, is the one place the operators appear as a vocabulary: it sets out the user's position that the 27 are the only commands anything is built with. A command there is a word a writer chooses and marks, not a cell this essay assigns, and whether the word fits the act stays a measured question.

1. What the pharos at the center is, and is not
-----------------------------------------------

The first Pharos was an island with no light on it. Prior art (Odyssey 4.351-361, read on Perseus). Menelaus tells Telemachus that on the way home he was held there for twenty days with no favourable wind, a day's sail off Egypt, at a place with a good harbour. Homer mentions no tower. The name came first, and the place was a waiting room for ships.

The tower came later, begun under Ptolemy I and finished under Ptolemy II around 280 BC, with dates that vary by source. Prior art (checked, hedged). Earthquakes damaged it, and by about 1480 a sultan had built a fort on the site from the fallen stone. I give no year for the fall.

The cloth word is spelled the same way. In the Odyssey Penelope's burial shroud for Laertes is the phaaros (2.97), the shroud itself a tapheion (2.99), made on a web called the histos (2.94). She weaves by day and unpicks by torchlight, for three years, and in the fourth one of the women who knew gives her away. Prior art (checked on Perseus, where the wording recurs at 19.138-147 and 24.128-137; line numbers came through a summarizing tool, so an offset of one line is possible). Whether the cloth word and the island name are related is not established. The cloth word has no agreed etymology, and the island's name is of uncertain origin. Prior art (checked, dictionary level). So the link is a pun, used openly. The cloth is the record, the weave. The light is the watching, the watch. Play.

Now the thing itself. The pharos is one shared append-only log. Entries are called wefts, and a weft is one line appended to a writer's own file: who wrote it, what it is about, what it says, and optionally an of link to the entry it follows. In the placed store an entry's id is a hash of the whole entry without its id, so it includes the writer, and the ids are those of the frozen library, the older reference library this project compares everything against. In the block store an id is a hash over kind, medium, codec, meta and payload (time is not hashed), so identical content gets one id there. Suppose two lanes, which are separate AI sessions, record different values for the same clip. The log keeps both values and their writers, and that is a fork. A retraction is a mark and never removes a value. Collapse is a choice a reader makes and is never written. Built (ledgers/pharos-lazy/API.md for the placed store, ledgers/pharos-blocks/FORMAT.md for the block store). No repository is meant to know any other, only the pharos, and an installer tree of 85 files carries three doors that wire a repo to it. Built (ledgers/pharos-public), installed only in sandbox homes.

One thing carries several names here, so fix them. The pharos is the whole design. Its stored record is the log, which stigmergy calls the medium and which this essay calls the cloth when it matters that the record is woven one line at a time. The watching of that record is the light. The hub, or the harbour, is the pharos seen as the place where agents arrive and leave marks.

Stigmergy is the name for coordination through such a log. Prior art. Pierre-Paul Grassé coined stigmergie in 1959, in a study of how two termite genera rebuild a damaged nest (Insectes Sociaux 6; checked, pages omitted because bibliographies disagree). Workers seem to act alone, yet the group builds coherent structure, because the work already done stimulates the next act. Heylighen (Cognitive Systems Research, 2016) splits the idea into an action, an agent, a medium, a trace and the coordination that follows when the trace stimulates a later act (checked on dblp and two summaries). Here the agent is any writer (a Premiere lane, a gateway, an extractor), the action is a write, the trace is a weft, the medium is the log, and the later act is a reader's ask. Proposal, as a reading of the design.

Which kind? Wilson's word sematectonic covers communication by changing the work itself, as a half-built wall tells the next builder where to build; a marker is a trace left apart from the work, like a scent. Theraulaz and Bonabeau (1999) split mechanisms into quantitative, where traces differ in intensity, and qualitative, where they differ in kind. Parunak crossed the two axes into four varieties. Prior art (secondary summaries only; no one person gets credit for the whole grid). By that grid a weft is a marker, and qualitative, since wefts differ in kind. A clip on a Premiere track is sematectonic, because the trace is the work, and a hit count on a scent trail (proposal 6) would be quantitative. Proposal.

Most of the design is refusal. The center does four small things: it appends, it keeps every value writers disagree about, it answers a question with pointers, and it checks every pointer against a content id before anyone relies on it. Everything a bus, a scheduler, a summary keeper or an authority would add is left out. No weft is addressed to an agent, because a trace needs only a place. The hub, which the design calls the Void, holds no queue, order or priority and gives no commands. Nothing in the medium says what the log means. Proposal, as stated design. The no-index stance has one piece of evidence: a mutant that writes an index file on weave was caught by the W suite, but only after the test author added a no-index-file scan because the first pass missed it. Result (test/EXPECTED-CHANGES.md; the 16 mutants ran against the author's naive mock, and I found no mutation run against the real source).

This essay makes no database claim. The decision ledger bars the words "database" and "beats SQLite" until stages 1 and 2 of the benchmark print a per-pattern WIN, TIE or LOSE verdict, and no such report is on disk (section 7). Result, by absence.

One mechanism in the project already fits the definition. The permit system for Premiere is a directory of flag files: a lane raises one, a grant answers it, a release clears it. A flag file is a trace in a shared medium, and it stimulates a later act by another agent. It is also a request addressed to the granter, so it is the borderline case: closer to a message than the wefts the design intends. Whether it counts as stigmergy depends on whether the granter acts on the flag itself or on being told. Proposal, as a reading. Over the replay there were 3,613 flag files and 1,132 raise-to-grant waits, with a median of 0.26 s and a maximum of 6.02 h. Result (out/t3.txt). The test built on them failed its own pass condition: its 10-minute threshold missed the 251-second wait it was meant to surface. Result.

Last, two lights. Proposal. A standing light shines for everyone and costs every crew its watch. An asked light shines only for the ship that asks, and the ship must know to ask. In the design, agents do not read the log. They ask, and receive pointers and bounded excerpts with "read N of M" stated. Proposal (the scale note concedes this is a convention, since the files are plain files). The placed store gives each ask exactly the files in its plan, and a second ask reads nothing. Built (API.md). The best hub probably offers both: a thin standing beacon saying only that something is there, which is the trail layer of proposal 6, and an asked light saying what. Proposal. Both lights assume the agent asks. That is untested. The one small model that has touched the adapter never called ask on the clip its question named (section 7), and if a harness or prompt forces the ask, then the harness is the stimulus and the trace is not. The stigmergic claim needs a reader that asks because of what a trace showed, which no record yet contains. Proposal.

2. What the other fields found, and what each paid
--------------------------------------------------

Nobody built this harbour from scratch. Each field kept some marks and paid in one currency, and the bill is the useful part. Each "bill" sentence below is my inference from the cited work, not a finding of its authors, and carries the Proposal tag of this paragraph even where a Prior art tag stands nearby. Proposal, as a reading.

Termites and the beach. Prior art. Grassé's stimulus was the work already done, and the bill is that nobody agrees what it is. Herbert Simon watched an ant cross a beach, taking a jagged path around pebbles, and said the path's complexity belongs to the beach while the ant stays simple (The Sciences of the Artificial, MIT Press, first published 1969, revised 1981 and 1996; I have not confirmed which edition first carries the ant; Simon offers it as a hypothesis with qualifications about memory and the inner environment). The bill the parable hides: the story works only if the ant's memory and inner life are set aside, which Simon himself qualifies.

The mill and the no-entry sign. Prior art. Army ants cut off from the column follow the ant ahead, each deposit reinforces the loop, and a closed trail carries no exit signal. Beebe described a large mill in Guiana (1921), and Schneirla published a formal study of circular milling in army ants (1944) (checked on Wikipedia; "lock-in" is my word, not theirs). The pharaoh's ant, Monomorium pharaonis, lays a repellent "no entry" trail on the unrewarding branch at a fork (Robinson, Jackson, Holcombe and Ratnieks, Nature 438, 2005; abstracts only). The bill for positive trails is a loop with no brake; the bill for negative ones is a second channel, a fork to mark, and receivers who trust a mark that says "nothing here."

Slime mould. Prior art. Physarum polycephalum finds the shortest route through a maze (Nakagaki and colleagues, Nature, 2000), and grown on a Tokyo-area map with oat flakes for cities it built a network that matched the rail system in cost, efficiency and fault tolerance (Tero and colleagues, Science, 2010). Tubes with more flow widen; tubes with little flow thin and vanish (checked). The medium is the animal's own body, so it is nearer sematectonic than marker-based. Proposal, as a reading. It cost: a rule that thins by flow will thin a path that is rarely used and critical on one bad day.

Sorting ants and the inherited soil. Prior art. Deneubourg and colleagues (conference 1990, proceedings 1991; authors from memory) modelled brood sorting: an unladen agent picks up isolated items, a laden one drops where like items are dense, and clusters form with no plan (Lumer and Faieta, 1994, added a similarity function). What it left unpaid: nothing says whether the clusters are the right ones. Odling-Smee, Laland and Feldman (2003) call the changes organisms hand down ecological inheritance, so descendants get the bad traces with the good (checked; contested as too broad).

Ant colony optimization and digital pheromones. Prior art. In Dorigo, Maniezzo and Colorni's Ant System (1996), ants deposit pheromone on edges in proportion to solution quality while all pheromone decays at a rate each round. Evaporation is the limiter on positive feedback, and the named failure is stagnation, every ant taking the same tour (checked; I did not read the stagnation section line by line). Evaporation alone does not cure it: Max-Min Ant System bounds the pheromone, and Ant Colony System lowers it on edges as ants cross. Parunak and colleagues gave software pheromones aggregation, evaporation and diffusion (1997 and 2002, cited together because I could not settle which first used the phrase). It cost: rates, bounds and weights that somebody chose.

Blackboards and tuple spaces. Prior art. In Hearsay-II (Erman, Hayes-Roth, Lesser and Reddy, 1980), knowledge sources post hypotheses to a shared blackboard while a separate focus-of-control mechanism schedules them. In Gelernter's Linda (1985), tuples persist independently of their producers and are matched associatively (checked as citations only). Hearsay-II paid with a scheduler, a steering center inside the hub. Linda paid by making matching the only language.

Storage. Prior art. Rosenblum and Ousterhout's log-structured file system (1991 and 1992) writes everything to a log and runs a cleaner that copies live data out of fragmented segments. A greedy cleaner does badly on skewed hot and cold data, and a cost-benefit policy did much better (as I know it from lecture notes). Bloom's filter (1970) may wrongly say "possibly present" and never wrongly says "absent," and it cannot delete (checked on Wikipedia and a CACM record; Bloom's text not read). In log-structured merge stores a delete is a tombstone that shadows older versions until compaction merges it with the data it covers (the combination is later than Bloom, as I recall). CRDTs converge by commutative operations or by a monotonic join on a semilattice (Shapiro and colleagues, 2011), and local-first software (Kleppmann and colleagues, 2019) argues that your own copy is the real one and sync is a merge (checked). The bills: a cleaner that spends bandwidth, extra reads, and removal, which union does not handle alone.

Ships, people and perceivers. Prior art. Hutchins's Cognition in the Wild (1995) studies navigation on a Navy amphibious ship he calls the Palau, a pseudonym, and takes the team plus its instruments as one cognitive system: bearings are taken, called, recorded, plotted and cross-checked by different people and tools (checked against his page and reviews). Elliott (2006) proposed wikis as stigmergic collaboration (checked). Gibson (1979) defined an affordance as what the environment offers an animal, a relation pointing at both (checked). Bateson's "a difference which makes a difference" is in his 1970 Korzybski lecture, reprinted in Steps to an Ecology of Mind (checked; no page cited). The bill, common to all of them: the weight sits on the perceiver, and a log keeps no perceiver unless someone writes one down.

The craft marks. Prior art. Cairns have marked routes for a very long time. Many parks now discourage building new ones, and cairns that pile up can mislead, which is why I use them here as a stigmergy example (checked lightly; the parks' stated reasons were not checked). In the Grimm version of 1812, Hansel's white pebbles lead the children home and his bread crumbs, on the second trip, are eaten by birds (checked). In Apollodorus, Ariadne's thread is fastened at the labyrinth's entrance and paid out behind Theseus (checked through summaries). The bills: crumbs get eaten, cairns multiply, and a thread can only lead back to the door where it was tied.

Two pairs, each a thought from science beside one from craft. First, Bateson and the shroud. A difference that makes a difference is the project's test for keeping a trace (proposal 7). Beside it, the suitors saw the day's weaving and did not learn of the nightly unpicking until, in the fourth year, one of the women who knew gave it away. Prior art (my paraphrase of the passages cited in section 1). Until then the unpicking changed what the suitors could see not at all and the outcome a great deal. Proposal, as a pairing. Second, Bloom and the cairn. A Bloom filter errs in one direction, and so does a cairn: it may mark a path that has washed out, and the cost is a longer walk. Proposal 3 is this rule in storage form. Proposal.

My sorting of these cases suggests, and does not prove, that the fields in this tour mostly kept the marks and paid in one of three currencies: a center that steers (Hearsay-II), a pile of tuned constants (the ants), or marks that outlive their meaning (cairns, crumbs). Proposal, as a reading. The design problem is to pay in a currency that can be measured. None of the analogies in this section, including the two pairs above, supports any proposal in section 4. They are where the ideas came from and no evidence that they work here. Only the tests in section 4 can support them. Proposal. The next section starts with the cloth because it is the half of the design where the bill has been counted. Proposal, as a reading.

3. The cloth: how the placed store finds things
-----------------------------------------------

On a loom the warp is the set of lengthwise threads held under tension, and the weft is the crosswise thread passed over and under them, one pass at a time, each pass a pick. Prior art (checked). The warp is settled before the first pick. To find the thread at a position on a finished cloth you do not consult a catalogue of the cloth. You go to the position.

The placed store follows that picture. An entry is placed by writer and by the first hex digit of the sha256 of its address, so a question about one address touches one shard per writer, and more where a hot shard has deepened. On disk there is PLACEMENT.json ({v:1, splitLimit:65536}) and files named <writer>/<shard>.jsonl, and no index, summary, checkpoint, cursor, lock or config. Built (ledgers/pharos-lazy/src, API.md). The split limit is a starting value, not tuned, and one writer per root is assumed. The warp is computed from the question, the weft is appended, and the log is both the full thing and the index, depending on how it is folded. Proposal, as a stance. The pieces under it are measured, as follows.

The weave cost stayed flat. Median append was 84.6 us at 1,000 entries and 85.7 at 1,000,000 (300 samples per size; 87.9 and 87.3 between), a largest-to-smallest ratio of 1.039 against a stamped bar of 3, and every returned id equalled the id the frozen library computes independently. Result (W1, out/results.json). The load average was near 57, and a weave never reads the log, so flat is what its construction predicts. It measures append and nothing about reading. The first weave of a kind in a process also reads its schema once, and that read is not in these medians.

Reads touched a bounded part of the log. On the real replay of 10,361,143 bytes, 174 cold asks gave 0 wrong answers and none over the shard-byte bound. The worst traced 8.173 percent of the log, and that address's own shard held 11.435 percent. On the synthetic log of 10^6 entries (344,349,067 bytes), 191 cold asks gave 0 wrong answers and the worst traced 1.62 percent. Result (W3). Caveats: the spec says 200 asks and the file shows 174 with no reason given; a 2-percent clause was loosened after the stamp for asks with large own shards; the 10^6 data is synthetic; "cold" means a fresh process over a warm file cache. The test can fail: a scanning reader over a shuffled placement broke the bound on 58 of 58 asks. Result.

Nearby results are narrower than they sound. A lazy state equalled a from-scratch fold on 240 (address, cut) pairs on the replay and 240 on a 10^5 log, with 0 mismatches. On the 10^5 log only 20 of the 240 pairs used a full-prefix reference; the rest used an address-restricted prefix fold. A fork-holding fold held 1,336 replay addresses as forks, where the frozen library reported 0 conflicts on the same entries, and the two counts answer different questions. Union merge of three parts (about 4,000 entries each) equalled a direct build in the three groupings tested, as a set of (path, id) pairs, with 1,543 addresses written by two or more writers, at a size where no address chain had split. Result (W2', W5', W7).

A hash puts strangers side by side. Two addresses in one shard have nothing to do with each other, so the placement computes a cost neighborhood and no meaning neighborhood. Meaningful neighborhoods would have to be earned from use, which is what proposal 6 would try to find out, since the inert medium cannot move an entry. Proposal.

The Jacquard machine, developed in France in the early 1800s, adds a notation: a chain of punched cards, one to a row of the pattern, decides which hooks lift. Prior art (checked). In the pharos the cards are schema entries: an ordinary entry at schema:<kind> says how that kind is placed. When two new kinds were given swapped schemas, the layout on disk swapped; PLACEMENT.json named no kind; and a static scan of six source files found no hard-coded kind name. Result (W14; the scan is a heuristic). No file other than the log and the reader's code says how to place the cloth, and the reader's code still fixes where schemas live (schema:<kind>) and which schema shapes it can parse. The schema shape inside a kind declaration is not fixed by the protocol (an open question flagged in ledgers/pharos-sqlite). Proposal, as the strongest form of the idea. The same design has a bill that the tests found, set out in proposal 5.

The log has weather. Collapse, the evaporation window and the shadow-read rate are, in the reader-private arm, parameters of a reader's projection and not written (in the woven arm the hints themselves are written), so two readers over one cloth see different surfaces. Call them weather. A dry spell is a narrow window and a flood a wide one. A storm is a day on which a reader throws away every trail and watches which come back. That is the hinge between the two halves of this essay: the cloth is the same for every reader, and the light is what each reader makes of it. Play.

4. Proposals for the best possible hub
--------------------------------------

Eleven proposals, in two groups. Proposals 1 to 5 concern the cloth, the record itself: what it refuses to store, what it keeps when writers disagree, how a reader may stop early without lying, and how the store finds its own first rule. Proposals 6 to 11 concern the light, the watching that leaves a trail for the next watcher: trails, forgetting, negative marks, reads that leave a trace, asking instead of reading, and the test of the whole claim. The cloth is the part with measurements. Each proposal gives what it is, the field that found the analogous thing, what it costs, its test or the statement that it is not yet pre-registered, and what it would teach about EO along with what would count against it. All are Proposal unless a tag says otherwise. I took the amendments' test identifiers second-hand, so check them in PREREG-A4.md to PREREG-A8.md.

1. Keep the medium inert. What: the inert rule of section 3, extended to every reader: collapse, decay, fold and trail evaluation happen at read time, and a reader's cache is private and disposable. Field: Linda's tuples outlive their producers, and Grassé's nest holds built work and no instructions. Prior art. Cost: every reader pays for what it folds. The block store's page table rebuilt at 10^6 blocks in 8,479 ms, and a region ask for one address in the disk layer read 7.2 percent of the log's bytes. Result (single runs, no bar). Test: the W suite and the index-file mutant of section 1. Teaches (a question, not a finding): whether an entry's role can be settled at read time from the asker's question and the log alone. Counts against: two readers asking the same question of the same entries settle different roles and nothing in the question or the log accounts for the difference; or any design that meets the W suite and block bars needs a stored summary that carries role information.

2. Keep every live value and say how many there are. What: when writers disagree, the fold keeps every live value, and the answer carries a survivor count: 1 determined, more than 1 held, 0 means the rules and the data disagree and is reported as a finding (A6 R-count). Field: Hearsay-II kept competing hypotheses on its board, as I recall, and paid for a scheduler to choose. Prior art. Cost: a held set can grow; A6 sets a fallback at 50 live values. Test: W5' and W8a passed on 99 planted forks and all 1,336 replay fork addresses, 0 values dropped and 0 invented. Result. On the replay this shows the lazy fold reproduces its reference held sets. It does not show that the 1,336 held addresses are real disagreements: the replay is one total causal chain and the frozen library finds 0 conflicts, so the forks may be an artifact of the head rule that reads of links alone. Only the 99 planted forks are known disagreements. A survivor count exists only in the wiring layer's engine adapter (mapHeld, which returns the live heads with survivors set to their true count, and whether the model sees an array or a number is undecided) and in the stub engine. Built (ledgers/pharos-wired, per its records; I did not re-run its tests). The A6 rules for the count are stamped, not built. Whether zero survivors ever arises from real gateway data is an open question (WIRING section 5). The replay is almost entirely single-writer-per-address chains, so it cannot exercise competing writers. Teaches (a question): what it means for one referent to carry two pasts. Counts against: on real data where a human adjudicator reads the held entries and finds one past, the survivor count is above 1 for most such addresses; or a model given the count scores no better on held-past questions than without it, by more than the stamped minimum effect, on either model.

3. Make every shortcut err toward reading more. What: a stop rule or filter may waste reads and may never end a walk too soon. A6's S4, "definitely absent," may wrongly say "maybe" and its false "no" count must be 0. Field: Bloom, 1970. Prior art. Cost: extra reads, and answers of "incomplete over range" where a gap might exist. Test: A6 R-oneside and A6-1 (at least 300 deep-past questions, where one wrong "no" falsifies), stamped, not built. The only measured case is a counterexample: ask(addr) without a kind returned a wrong region for 20 of 50 addresses that span a schema change, ruled a real defect and unfixed. Result (W13.addr-span). Teaches (a question): whether "I could not tell" can be a first-class answer. Counts against: any false "no" in the A6-1 set (stamped as falsifying), or a stop rule whose false-"maybe" rate is as high on the rule's own planted-gap controls as on a coin-flip rule, so that nothing separates it from luck.

4. Read the rules first, then fold backward from the newest entry. What: resolve the schema as of the log position being asked about (A5), then walk from the newest entries and stop on a superseding record, a closed held set or a prefix entailed by of links alone (A4 S1 to S3). Field: log-structured merge reads go newest first and let tombstones shadow older versions, as I recall. Prior art. Cost: the rules read is counted in every measurement. Today every reader opens the union over all schema versions, and the post-change cost bound failed in all four lanes. Result (W13). Test: A4 and A5: the backward fold must give the same answers as the full fold on at least 1,000 questions while touching under 5 percent of the log at the median on hot addresses. Stamped, not built. Teaches (a question): whether the distance from the tail to a stop point predicts which entries an answer needs. Counts against: the equivalence check fails; the median touched ratio is not below 0.05 on hot addresses; or the entries inside the stop point are no more often needed by an independent answer key than entries outside it.

5. Let the store find its own first schema. What: weave computes the "no prior" case when the first schema of a kind is woven, so no caller types it, and (a separate question) whether writers could nominate kinds. Field: the Jacquard card, a swappable chain read by one machine. Prior art. Cost: stale-schema writers place entries by the old schema, and a first-schema lookup is needed per kind per process, which W1 did not include. Without a typed prior:'none', writer-stream reads exceeded the bound on 20 of 20 reads (maximum 5.703 times the bound) and time-window reads on 24 of 24 (maximum 46.704 times). Result (W11), ruled a real defect, a stage-2 item with no test of its own. Test: W11 to W13 are stamped; W12 passed 9 of 10 sub-checks, the tenth only under a post hoc ruling. The wiring layer's five event kinds were typed by its author, who flags that as a breach of the earned-never-typed rule. Built (ledgers/pharos-wired). Teaches: nothing about cells. It tests only whether a placement rule can be derived from the log without a caller typing a flag. Counts against: after the change W11 R2 or R3 still exceed their bounds, or the derived default is wrong for a kind whose first schema a second writer wove.

6. Scent trails with no authority. What: a reader whose read succeeds leaves a hint: where it found the answer, the id as the check, a hit count, a log position (A7). A hint orders where to look and answers nothing. It is verified on every use, reinforced when it verifies, and may be wrong. Two arms are stamped: reader-private, and hints woven as entries that other agents follow, the stigmergic arm. That other agents follow woven hints is an assumption; no record shows any agent does. Field: Dorigo's pheromone, Parunak's digital pheromones, Ariadne's thread against the eaten crumbs. Prior art. Cost: a failed hint costs a read, trails can harden into checkpoints (section 6), and trails may hold absolute local addresses (A7 permits an absolute address as a hint), which would keep them out of a release tree. Test: PREREG-A7.md sets three checks: answers must equal the forward fold under planted bad trails, trails must save reads on Zipf-shaped question streams, and a cold start may cost at most 5 percent extra. Stamped, not built. Teaches (a question): whether usage alone can earn a sense of which addresses belong together. Counts against: the Zipf check shows no saving over the no-trail baseline; the saving appears only on the synthetic Zipf streams, which are built with repeat structure, and not on the first real stream; the clusters the trails form are fully explained by query frequency; or answers under planted bad trails differ from the forward fold.

7. Evaporate by the difference a trail makes, and watch for the mill. What: A8. A trail stays only while it saves reading, net of verification and deposit cost. Savings are measured by shadow reads on a deterministic 5-percent sample (question id hash mod 20), with leave-one-out credit when several trails served one question. The window over past uses widens until the keep-or-drop decision stops changing, so no half-life is typed. Field: evaporation in Ant System, tube atrophy in Physarum, the army ant mill, and Bateson's difference. Prior art. Cost: the repeated reads are pure overhead, and a short window thrashes. Test: A8 DV1 to DV7 and its mutants, stamped, not built, with a prediction that can fail: trails survive mainly for addresses far from the tail, and hot-address trails die. A fixed-window arm stays as comparator. The same shadow reads might also flag a mill, but that is untested: a trail that always verifies and saves nothing is useless, and a mill is a positive-feedback loop, which is not the same thing (DV6, a planted useless trail must score at or below zero, and DV7, thrash counted, cover the first only; a dedicated mill test is not yet pre-registered). Teaches (a question): forgetting as a measured difference, and whether use and usefulness can be told apart from the log alone. Counts against: the fixed-window comparator arm keeps trails and saves reads as well as A8 within the stamped margin, in which case the measured-difference rule adds nothing over counting use, and use and the difference a trail makes cannot be told apart on this log; or hot-address trails survive. The savings measure is a cost proxy and borrows Bateson's phrase without implementing his concept.

8. Give the hub a way to say "nothing here." What: refusals, errors, in-flight and unanswered calls become entries, and a no-entry weft records "I looked here and found it unrewarding," with its range and log position. It may demote a region in the read order and may never skip one, so it obeys proposal 3. Field: the pharaoh's ant, and the tombstone, a delete that outlives its target. Prior art. Cost: negative traces accumulate, and a wrong mark closes a branch for everyone if it may skip. Test: the gateway capture classes call outcomes as ok, error, in_flight, jsonrpc_error, refused and unanswered. Built (ledgers/gateway-capture), failure tests 10 of 10 against a fake upstream, synthetic. This is not the no-entry weft, which is unbuilt. End to end, the existing tailer and ask handled the new events poorly (T8, 8 of 13 checks): of 208 events the tailer ingested 188 and rejected 20 as not one of four declared shapes, in_flight showed for 3 of 3 job ids, refused outcomes were not shown, and ask found 0 of 10 reads. Result, synthetic. The gap is between the new event types and the older consumers, and nobody has decided which side to change (an Undecided item, section 7). Reading a no-entry mark as a signal to the next reader is not yet pre-registered. Teaches (a question): whether a failed attempt, a retracted claim and an absent entry are one kind of trace or three. I assign nothing. Counts against treating them as three: givers' Same or Other calls on real traces do not separate them any better than the calls within each group.

9. Let reading leave a trace, and give the perceiver a window. What: record read digests (bytes and hash, never the body), created ids, failure classes and the acting lane at the moment of the call. Add the perceiver's window as its own named projection, "what a perceiver who sees only these writers could know," which differs from "values written by these writers." Field: Hutchins's cross-checked fix cycle, and Gibson's relational affordance. Prior art. Cost: a bigger log and a privacy edge. The p95 latency difference over 1,000 calls was -0.07, -0.12 and +0.75 ms in three replications against a 5.0 ms bar, and a separate real-size run failed at +5.13 ms in one of two. Result. Test: the capture is Built (transparency 6 of 6 on the newest re-run, where the earlier recorded run had 5 of 6; created ids 17 of 17; reads 12 of 12; lane 16 of 16; mutants M1 to M10 caught in the final serial reruns, with T1, T2 and T9 run against M1 only, M4 caught by one test that is flaky under load, and one earlier summary recording M4 as survived; all on synthetic data against a fake upstream) and is not installed live: the live gateway matches the frozen baseline and runs {"dry": true}. The forward test (at least 400 real writes, created ids at or above 95 percent against a 77 percent baseline) is stamped and unrun. The window has no test of its own. W8e shows that two defensible readings of a writer-subset window disagree on 52 of 247 checked addresses, and that a perceiver window is the right fix is a reading, not a measurement. Result. On the replay "since I looked" cannot be tested at all, because every write has one predecessor. Result. Teaches (a question): whether reading should be perceiver-relative. Counts against: on a forward test with logged reads, answers from the perceiver window do not differ from answers from the writer-subset filter on questions about what the asker could have known, so the distinction is idle.

10. Agents are meant to ask, not read. What: the asked light of section 1 as an interface: a question goes in, and pointers and bounded excerpts come back with "read N of M." Agents are meant to ask and not read (a convention, since the files are plain files). Field: Simon's ant, a thin agent in a rich environment, and Hearsay-II's split of knowledge from control. Prior art. Cost: the answering layer owns what counts as an excerpt, and the small model may simply fail. Test: the intelligence test (ledgers/pharos-intelligence/PREREG.md, amendments A1 to A3). Its claim C: a small local model shown only pointers and bounded excerpts answers as well as with full context where that fits, and each of three read-side features (rules read, held pasts, scent trails) makes a measurable difference. The harness is Built, and its stub self-test passed: 488 stub questions, harness mutants caught 18 of 18. Result, machinery only; section 7 gives the one real contact. Teaches (a question): whether a small model given a survivor count answers held-past questions better than without it. Counts against: on either model (verdicts never pooled) the H-on accuracy on held-past questions is not above H-off by more than the stamped minimum effect, or the model treats a count above 1 as an answer.

11. Test the stigmergy itself. What: the claim that one agent's entries change what another reads or does. The result that matters is a pre-registered effect over a stamped number of cases where a second agent's reading is cheaper, or better, because a first agent was there, on non-synthetic traffic, with the traces removed as the control. Field: Heylighen's definition makes that stimulus the whole point. Prior art. Cost: real traffic from at least two real writers, logged reads (proposal 9), and a blind question author. Test: A7's woven arm is the nearest stamped piece, on synthetic Zipf streams. A two-agent test on real traces is not yet pre-registered. Teaches (a question): whether the traces do work that the model alone does not. Counts against stigmergy here: with the first agent's traces removed, or replaced by a shuffled agent's, the second agent's reads cost the same and its answers score the same; or an effect appears on synthetic streams and vanishes on real ones. If trails change cost and not answers, that is a stigmergic effect on reading cost and says nothing about whether the hub steers nobody.

5. Play
-------

Everything in this section is Play, except where a Prior art tag marks a cited fact. None of it is a claim. Each item takes one mechanism from sections 3 and 4, asks what it becomes if the myth is taken literally, and where it can, ends with something that could be tried.

The maids' lane. One writer whose only job is to witness: it folds the weaving and the unpicking together and writes the net. A reader who wants to know what the day wove and the night undid asks that lane. In the Odyssey one of the women who knew gave the weaving away (2.108).

The bed in the tree. Odysseus proves himself with a bed built around a living olive, movable only by cutting the trunk at the root. Prior art (checked on Perseus). He calls it the sema, the sign, and Penelope's knees give way (Od. 23.177-206). A trail's content id is the olive: anyone can say where the answer was, and only a holder of the entry can reproduce the bytes. Rehash is the bed test: recompute the hash from the bytes and see whether it matches the id.

The lamp that steers nobody. A lamp entry carries no content, only a cursor. Its brightness is computed by each reader, at read time, from the number of verified trails that pass through it, so nothing is stored and only what a reader can check counts. A ship sees it from far off and decides for itself, and a lamp nobody re-verifies goes dark. The risk is that brightness steers by popularity, which is the mill again.

The ledger of ships that never came. A keeper's book lists the ships that passed. Keep the other book, as in proposal 8, but write it for the keeper, not the ships: a daily page listing the asks that found nothing, so the keeper can see where the harbour's coverage ends.

Warp, pick and hand. Take addresses as warp and each writer's pass as a pick, as in section 3, and let a question be the weaver's hand that decides which threads to lift in this one reading. A fold is cloth woven on demand, and a schema change is a re-warping. The Jacquard card of section 3 stays the schema entry.

Ariadne's thread is an of link. From any weft you can follow of back toward the entrance. Would a lost agent find its way more cheaply by following the chain than by searching?

The fort made of the lighthouse. Compaction folds old writer files into newer ones, as the sultan folded the fallen tower into his fort. The test is plain: after compaction, does every old citation still resolve to its bytes?

Oat flakes for the hub. Feed the hub a question stream shaped like a map and see whether the trails that survive A8 redraw the map, as Tero's mould redrew Tokyo's rails.

Sorting without moving. Deneubourg's ants sort by carrying items. The inert medium cannot move an entry, so give the job to the trails: each reader who finds two addresses useful in one session leaves a link between them, and the links, not the bytes, form the clusters. The test is whether the clusters match the ones a person draws by hand.

A shroud woven to be late. Give a held fork a clock: it may stay a fork for at most a stated span of log time, and a reader who must act on it sooner has to say which value it is betting on, in an entry of its own. The suitors are the readers who want one value now, and the bets are how they get it while the cloth stays unfinished. The test is whether bets cluster on one value.

A cairn with a cap. Allow at most k trails per region, and let a new trail replace the one that has saved the least. Watch which regions end up with none.

An open question I will not answer. Deposit, verify, reinforce and evaporate are four things a reader does to a trail. Whether any of them is an act of the same kind as one of the nine operators is an open question, and no mapping is offered. Cells are earned from data by a Same-versus-Other test and nominated by the givers. If the project ever holds trail data, the test is to see what the acts cluster into, and to be ready to find nothing the nine operators name.

6. Failure modes and attacks
----------------------------

Each item is a Proposal about a risk unless a tag says otherwise, with the project's evidence where it exists.

Lock-in, of two kinds. The first is the mill: positive feedback has no brake of its own, and proposal 7 carries the detector, stamped and unbuilt. The second is format lock-in. The first schema of a kind shapes every later read, since readers open the union over every schema version and at is informational and cannot prune it. Result (W13; the cost is real and deferred to A5).

Poisoning. A hint is meant to order where to look and answer nothing. If the id check is implemented and a reader falls back to a full walk on failure, a false hint should cost attention and not correctness. That is design intent (A7, stamped, not built) and is untested. An id check does not catch a hint that verifies but points to a real entry that is stale, superseded or irrelevant. A false no-entry mark closes a branch if a reader lets it skip, which is why proposal 8 forbids the skip. A8's sample is also a weakness. A sample chosen by question-id hash mod 20 is deterministic, so anyone who knows the rule knows which questions will be double-checked and can behave well on exactly those. That is my reading and no finding, and it argues for a sample salted by something the writer cannot see. Nor can retraction unwrite poison: a retraction is a mark, so the poisoned entry stays in the log with a mark beside it. That is the intended behavior, and also the cost.

Authority creep, where a trail becomes a checkpoint. A path worn across a field is both a record of where people walked and an instruction to the next walker, and the dirt does not tell them apart. Play. In the pharos the risk is concrete. A7 says trails have no authority and are verified on every use, and that if in practice they harden into trusted checkpoints, that is the finding. The defense is the content-id check, and the measure is how often a verification fails. If it never fails, either the hints are good or nobody is checking, and the log must be able to tell which. Proposal.

Goodhart on the difference that makes a difference. Once savings measured by shadow reads become what a writer is rewarded for, writers can make trails that save reading as measured: depositing only on easy questions, or splitting a trail into pieces that each look marginal. Leave-one-out credit is stamped to attribute savings among trails that served one question. Whether it resists gaming is untested, and I do not see that it resists depositing only on easy questions. A8 is a measure, and it is likely to be gamed once a writer has a reason to game it.

Privacy of paths. Trails may hold absolute local addresses (A7 permits an absolute address as a hint), which would keep them out of a release tree, and read digests can leak what was read even without the body. The public scan of the release tree scanned 85 files and gave one finding, exit 1: the 28 MB live log folder in the working folder (13 files holding third-party transcript text and mirrored private logs), kept out of version control only by a * gitignore. Result (PUBLIC-READINESS.md). The addressable-disk note says private data needs a separate volume. Proposal.

Hostile and sloppy writers. Sloppy is the common case. In 1,134 of 6,542 permit-carrying rows more than one permit was active, so the logged "who," the first active permit, is wrong on those rows. The guard's pre.jsonl listed 6,736 allowed writes against 6,611 log rows, and the capture plan counts 249 allowed calls (3.7 percent) that never reached the log. That 249 is not the difference of the two counts (125), and I have not reconciled the two figures. The write counts in this essay (6,542, 6,568, 6,611 and 6,736) come from different filters over one replay, and I did not reconcile them. Result (out/t5.json, CAPTURE-PLAN.md). A cross-check of 30 build-log sentences against the replayed writes matched 12, or 0.40 against a bar of 0.70, which failed; 12 of the 30 named a lane that is not a real writer name. Result (T2).

A real log with distinct writer roles, the Ellison statements log, shows what a second writer is for: 764 entries from three writers (scout 37, extractor 474, verifier 253), in which the verifier returned 45 failures against 202 extracted statements. Result (ledgers/ellison-statements/LEDGER.md, under its own tier rule, a ruling and no proof of who spoke). Its roles were assigned by a pipeline, so it is not evidence of stigmergic coordination, and whether the verifier's 45 failures are correct was not checked independently. It shows only that a second writer can disagree with a first in the log, and the ledger does not measure whether the verifier coordinated through the log or by instruction. A verifier that checks the extractor's entries and disagrees is the kind of second writer the hub needs, and it is one more writer who can be wrong. Proposal.

Many writers also meet the lock question. The placed store has no locks and assumes one writer per root, and a split-chain merge may fail by the predictions file (EXPECTED-REAL.md P4) and is untested. In the SQL write path, two processes inserting 200 rows each landed 330 of 400 entries, with 20 "database is locked" errors from the SQLite fold step while the block layer verified clean. Result (the pharos-blocks writes test W9, failed; lib/disk.mjs was modified after this run, and whether it was fixed is not recorded).

An address is not a permission. Content addressing says which bytes. It says nothing about who may read them. The SQL projection serves read-only on 127.0.0.1 behind a lexical guard, a read-only open and an authorizer: 296 fuzzed write or escape statements, 0 accepted, and one benign statement refused by design. Result (P9). The disk layer's local HTTP has no authentication, and its own notes say an address is still not a permission. A capability design, where a pointer is paired with the right to follow it, is open and unbuilt. Proposal. A pointer handed out through ask promises nothing but the bytes.

8. Twenty-seven commands
------------------------

Proposal (the user's, 2026-11-14). The pharos is meant to have exactly 27 commands, and nothing else counts as a command. They are the nine operators NUL, SIG, INS, SEG, CON, SYN, DEF, EVA and REC, each in three grains. The bare name is Figure, the thing in front of you. A minus goes down to Ground, the declarations and schema underneath. A plus goes up to Pattern, what is built on top and written back. So INS- kind:clip declares a kind, INS clip:7 makes a clip, and INS+ view:open writes a view back. The notation is prefix: the command comes first, its arguments follow, and commands nest, as in EVA SEG- x. An argument may be only an address, an entry id, a literal value or another command. There are no clause keywords, no settings, no function library and no separate way to write a condition. These 27 are meant to be the only commands anything is built with: every door speaks them, every projection (a SQL table, an Obsidian vault, the excerpt in a small model's context) is a nest of them, and data can flow any way because a conversion is itself one of these forms.

Built (khora/native/docs/THE-27-CELLS.md, whose cell assignments are generated by walking the cube in eoreader7/native/kernel/cube.js). Each of the 27 commands also has a terrain and a stance, and neither is a second thing to write. An operator is a pair of a mode and a domain. The modes are Differentiate (cut apart), Relate (put beside) and Generate (bring into being). The domains are Existence (NUL, SIG, INS), Structure (SEG, CON, SYN) and Interpretation (DEF, EVA, REC). A terrain is a domain at a grain, the kind of place an act lands on: Void, Entity and Kind for Existence; Field, Link and Network for Structure; Atmosphere, Lens and Paradigm for Interpretation. A stance is a mode at a grain, the kind of act it is: Clearing, Dissecting and Unraveling for Differentiate; Tending, Binding and Tracing for Relate; Cultivating, Making and Composing for Generate. So the mark on a command already says both: INS- lands on Void as Cultivating, INS on Entity as Making, INS+ on Kind as Composing, and NUL on Entity as Dissecting. Each terrain holds three commands, one per mode, and each stance holds three, one per domain. The rule that comes with them is that a cell classifies a move, never content.

Result and Built (ledgers/pharos-eoql/SPEC.md, SPEC-A1.md, SPEC-A2.md). The language this replaces was larger. The written specification had 9 verbs, 8 frame arguments, 5 extra primitives (fixpoint, a term language, parameters, labels and a write marker) and 3 closed vocabularies of functions. Amendment A2 held all 27 positions, but through a grain: keyword and a fourth vocabulary. A partial build reached a first test run that was never finished: 27 of 31 strict matches against the older v1 language, 206 of 228 SQL queries translated, and 148 of 1,124 format-flow checks failed. The build was parked when the work turned to inventing the language from what the project has learned. The 27-command rule removes everything that specification added beyond the operators themselves.

Proposal (the user's). All of mathematics is meant to come from the same 27: arithmetic, geometry and integral calculus included. Here is what that could look like, and it is Play, a direction and not a construction anyone has checked; the interpreter run described below built part of it, with different readings. A number is a chain of INS, each new entry of the one before, so that counting is walking the chain. Addition is SYN of two chains, and taking away is SEG, cutting one chain at another. Order is CON along the chain and comparison is EVA of two chains. A point is an INS, a line is a CON between two points, a figure is a SYN of lines, and congruence is EVA of two figures under REC. An integral is SEG cutting an interval finer and finer, SYN summing the parts, and DEF+ fixing the pattern the sums approach. Counts against: any piece of this needs a symbol from outside the 27, such as a literal number that carries its own order and arithmetic, so that the mathematics is imported in the literals and not built from the commands; or two different operations, such as sum and difference, can only be told apart by naming them.

The test. The claim is pre-registered in ledgers/pharos-eoql/PREREG-27.md (sha256 c8037b91, stamped 2026-11-14T19:52:45Z), written before any answer came in. It is falsified by any one of five things, each taken from real work and not invented: an operation that cannot be written at all (F1), one that needs something added outside the list (F2), two different operations forced into one form (F3), a meaning that rests on an unwritten default (F4), or a conversion that loses what no 27-command form can carry back (F5). Every session working on the project that day was asked to try to break it. Proposal.

Result (seven sessions. All first answered as design input from their own code; two then built evaluators for the 27 forms and ran them, described below). Each session first answered on its own reading, and four then revised after the user ruled on points of doctrine in their sessions. Both counts are kept, because the pre-registration counts a fit reached by a ruling as a fit with a stretch until it is written and run. Counts are given as fit, fit with a stretch, counterexample.

- fixed-context apparatus (budgets, instruments, excerpts): first answer 9, 5, 3; after the rulings, 9, 8, 0.
- Wilson's ants (marks, trails, evaporation): first answer 5, 6, 5 classes over 7 operations; after the rulings, not revised.
- intelligence test (23 asks): first answer 6, 10, 7 rows in 5 classes; after the rulings, not revised.
- block store, SQL projection, Same/Other comparator: first answer 8, 7, 3; after the rulings, 3 withdrawn as misreadings, none written.
- pharos log side (SQL door, codecs, Wikidata plan): first answer 5 classes, plus SQL as a source language; after the rulings, all withdrawn; a canon conflict and a naming conflict remain.
- main pharos session (folds, installer, as-of cuts): first answer 9, 6, 5; after the rulings, 14, 7, 2 after building an evaluator.
- text finder and Obsidian (33 operations): first answer 15, 9, 9 rows in 6 classes; after the rulings, 10, 14, 9 rows in 6 classes, after the ruling on NUL moved five fits to stretches.
- bounded-context local model (an interpreter, 56 forms run): first answer 2 counterexamples, 4 stretch patterns; after the rulings, 1 counterexample, 5 stretch patterns; its NUL readings later ruled wrong.

Result (ledgers/eoql-27-demo, README.md and scratch/demo-output.txt; one author for code and demo, no independent check). One session built what no other had: an interpreter that parses only the 27 forms, prefix with arguments that are an address, a numeral, a literal or a nested command. It ran 56 forms, used all 27, and left the real Ellison ledger unchanged. Its kernel does five things: make things, merge them by address, cut, follow of links, and count. On those it built arithmetic and some calculus. Addition is CON, subtraction NUL (a reading the user has since ruled wrong, below), multiplication SYN+ of REC, division DEF as an exact ratio of measures, equality EVA, and Pythagoras checks. The integral of x from 0 to 1 came out 11/20, 101/200 and 1001/2000 at 10, 100 and 1,000 cuts. The integral of x squared came out 77/200 and 6767/20000 at 10 and 100 cuts. The derivative of x squared at 1 came out 21/10, 201/100 and 2001/1000. The limit is EVA against a baseline, the difference that makes no difference. Proposal (the Obsidian session's, worked out on the user's erasure ruling and checked against the pharos specification; nothing built). DEF x VOID removes x's payload wherever the log lives, leaves a tombstone in its place, and is itself an ordinary entry with of x, a byline, a time and a reason. Unlike retraction, which keeps the content and can be undone, a void cannot be undone, and a tombstone cannot itself be voided, because it is the proof of erasure. A nested cut voids many things at once: DEF (CON person:p) VOID voids everything traced from one person. The effect ripples down of links and never down seen, since seen records what came before, not what depended on it, and following it would void everything written afterward. What happens to a dependent depends on its kind, and each kind's declaration says which: a quotation is voided, a fold over many things is refolded without x, and a judgement that rested on x keeps its place but its standing becomes "could not be said". A kind that says nothing is voided, and that default is written in the protocol's own declaration so it is not hidden. The void reports its own frontier, as a verdict does: where the erasure reached and where it could not, such as a person's own note file, a backup not yet expired, or a model's context or a session transcript that has already read x. An erasure is only as strong as its search, so it says what it searched. Five problems stand between this and the log as built. First, an entry's id is the hash of its content, so a tombstone that keeps the other fields lets anyone confirm a guessed short body; the options are a salt destroyed at the void, a tombstone that drops fields, or, as I recall from practice elsewhere, crypto-shredding, where each body has its own key and erasing destroys the key. Second, an address or a byline can itself be personal. Third, merging is a grow-only union, so any copy that still holds the full line brings it back, unless a tombstone always beats a full entry and a void may rewrite a line in another writer's file. Fourth, a derived entry that does not name its source in of leaks, so doors must refuse such entries. Fifth, "VOID" is already a name twice over, for the hub and for khora's empty extent, though in canon the Void is the Ground of Existence and the user may mean that Void on purpose. These questions have gone to the sessions that own storage, folds and the write door. Counts against: a real dependent for which voiding, refolding and marking are all wrong; or a merge rule that cannot keep a voided body from returning.

Proposal, refinements from four sessions (the text finder, the ants, the block store and the main pharos session, still in progress; nothing built). Seven points have converged. (1) Erase is DEF x VOID, and its preview is NUL (DEF x VOID), which holds, changes nothing, and returns what the erase would reach: the dependents by of, each with its rule, and the doors, copies and backups that would have to confirm. The erase names the preview it accepted and is refused if the dependents have changed since, so an erase is never run blind. (2) NUL is the look-only wrapper, and a bare top-level form writes. (3) There are three removals with three homes: erasing is final and lives on DEF; un-saying is reversible and is proposed for REC, since the entry stays and only how it stands changes; and finding what is absent is a cut on the void, SEG (…) VOID, which only works if each hole is a typed entry with a kind and a reason, because the finder already needs four absences kept apart (asked with no answer, nobody to ask, not asked because the answer could not change, left out by the question's shape) and an erasure is a fifth. (4) A tombstone keeps the cell, the kind and the links and drops the body, and drops the address too when the kind declares that its addresses can carry content (name:<ref>, trail:<holder>). Bodies are crypto-shredded, and the key store is never replicated or backed up the way the log is, or restoring a backup undoes the erase. (5) The ripple follows of only, and a refolded entry must not keep the voided id in its own of. (6) A piece of content has one home: every other entry points at it and never copies it, an address is never the content, and a door refuses a derived entry with no of. This is "agents ask for pointers, not content" applied to entries, and with it the ripple is complete by construction and one key shreds the only copy. What still needs its own rule is whatever transforms content without quoting it: a reading, an embedding, a model's answer. (7) An erase returns its frontier, naming every place it could not reach. The write door, Penelope, admits an erase on its own route, the pharos library carries it out under a lock, and if the door is down the erase waits and reports; it never goes around her.

Proposal, the list amended (the text finder, after the block store's six cases and the ants' correction; and the main pharos session's answers on storage and folds; design input, not legal advice). The outcomes for a dependent become four. Void removes it. Replace voids an old aggregate and then folds a new one whose of no longer names the voided id, because old and new side by side give the erased item away: for a mean over ten batches, x is ten times the old mean minus nine times the new one, exactly, and a median or quantile is bounded the same way. Mark may be declared only by a kind that can hold none of x's content; anything that can carry a value, a name, an excerpt or a summary must void or replace. Release runs the other way, from a thing to its parts: a chunk of media shared by several manifests is voided when its last standing holder is. A voided participant leaves its act standing but unbound: "Globex said Acme lost" becomes an act whose loser is a hole, which a reader takes as a gap, never as the weaker claim "Globex said someone lost". References inside a body become typed values that the write door copies into of itself, since today two stores keep references in the body with of empty, so a ripple down of would reach nothing. The main pharos session favours crypto-shredding over a salt or a stripped tombstone, for four reasons: the ciphertext stays, so every entry's id still verifies and every fold's lossless check still passes; no writer's file is ever rewritten, so the log stays append-only; a stripped tombstone does not stop guessing, because kind and address are usually known and the time can be narrowed to a window of about a hundred seconds; and the voided entry keeps its place in causal order, so as-of cuts, positions and declared orders do not silently shift. Addresses that can be personal are placed by a keyed hash. Key destruction travels as an ordinary void entry to every copy, and a copy that never receives it keeps its key, which no scheme fixes. Derived state that is not an entry is never reached by a ripple: installed folds and caches. The vector fold's cache is keyed by a hash of each entry's text, so after a void it keeps the embedding and its key confirms a guessed text. So installs must be registered in the log with what they contain, a void must force each one to update and shred, and caches must be keyed by entry id, never by content. Embeddings carry content, since text can often be partly recovered from them, so an embedding of x is voided, not marked. A SQL projection is not erased by deleting rows and compacting: the database must overwrite freed pages, its write-ahead journal must be truncated, and earlier rebuilt copies removed. The frontier gains three classes: copies outside the dependency graph (retired segments, quarantine, projections and their journals, staging and test copies, backups, other sessions' mirrors), which needs a written inventory for each store; influence already spent; and anything earned on a population that included x, such as a ground, a threshold or a measured score, which has to be earned again. And one leak touches this project's own method: every pre-registration stamp is a hash over files that may hold text, so before an erase it confirms a guess and after one it can no longer be checked; stamps over hold-out data should be taken over salted or keyed ids. The block store adds four points from its own build. Its large objects share chunks whose ids are hashes of their bytes alone, so a salt per entry would destroy the deduplication chunks exist for; there a key held per store, or per data subject, keeps deduplication inside the store while stopping outsiders from confirming that it holds a given text. With encryption, retired segments and quarantined copies can keep their ciphertext; without it they would have to be destroyed, which contradicts the store's rule that no operation deletes or rewrites a block. Its integrity check must accept "voided: body unreadable, id kept" as a valid state, not as corruption. And the same bytes arriving from two sources are two entries with two ids, so a void by id erases one copy, not the content. A void must also be a kind that no retraction can name, since retracting a retraction restores its target and a void must never be restored. Not settled: what a tombstone keeps. The text finder would keep only the id, the of links and the void's byline, since address, writer and time can all carry content; the main session would keep the place in order as well. Under crypto-shredding both can hold: the encrypted entry keeps its place and its id, and nothing readable remains. The ruling that decides the rest is the user's: whether ids are made unlinkable to content, so that the same content arriving again gets a new id and erasure goes by subject, or whether every copy is found by its content. The text finder and the main session both argue for one home for content and erasure by subject.

The integral is an identity question. Proposal (the user's, 2026-11-14). An integral is precisely the asymptotic problem of identity solved. The staircase at ten cuts is not the triangle, nor at a hundred, nor at a thousand. At no finite step are they the same thing. The integral is the answer to the question when they may be taken as the same: when one more refinement makes no difference that matters. That is the run's own limit form, EVA of the area at 1,000 cuts against 1/2, which answers "is not" on its own, with the exact difference 1/2000. The tolerance is not an extra argument but a DEF of that EVA (the user's correction): DEF measures the difference in a declared unit, here 1/1000, itself built as DEF (INS 1000) (INS 1), and less than one whole unit is a difference that makes no difference. So the baseline is written in the same commands as everything else. So the integral sits on the same mechanism as two other pieces of this project. The Same/Other question asks when two readings are one thing. The backward fold's stop rule asks when reading further could no longer change the answer. Both are settled by a difference that makes no difference, measured against a declared baseline, and recorded as a revisable event. If the user's position holds, these are one act: identity reached in the limit, under a ground that says what difference counts. This is why the missing automatic limit (K2) matters beyond calculus. A form that refines until no difference is the same form the fold needs to stop and the comparator needs to call two things the same. Counts against: the limit form gives different answers along different refinement paths (cutting into halves against cutting into thirds) under the same baseline, so the identity it reaches depends on the path and not on the thing; or the baseline that makes the staircase equal the triangle cannot be the same kind of ground event as the one Same/Other and the fold already declare.

What a proof would take. Proposal. "Any form of mathematics" can mean three different things, and each needs a different proof.

First, compute anything that can be computed. Prior art (as I recall, not checked here). Kleene showed that the computable functions are exactly those built from zero, successor, projection, composition, primitive recursion and an unbounded search; Minsky showed a machine with two counters that can be incremented, decremented and tested for zero is already universal. The run has zero (the empty chain), successor (INS), composition (nesting) and a zero test (EVA against nothing). It lacks recursion and search, which is K2 again. The proof is to add one recursion form inside the 27, then compile a known universal model, such as a two-counter machine, into the 27 and run its standard programs, and to state the encoding precisely enough that a second person could check it, ideally in a proof assistant.

Second, build the number tower. Natural numbers are chains. Integers are pairs of chains, and rationals are already in the run as measures. Reals are not finite objects. The constructive route (Bishop's, as I recall) treats a real as a rule of refinement together with a bound on its error, which is exactly a pattern of refinements judged by EVA against a declared baseline. The proof has to show that the field laws (commutativity, associativity, distribution, inverses) hold under the declared readings, first by testing them over many values and then by argument, and that two refinement rules for the same real are judged the same. π, e and the square root of 2 are the test cases; π needs the area of a circle, which no run has yet computed.

Third, prove theorems, not only compute values. Showing that the staircase approaches 1/2 for every number of cuts, not only the three that were run, is an induction over all of them, and that needs a proof system, not an evaluator. Prior art (as I recall). Gödel showed that no consistent system strong enough for arithmetic proves every true statement of arithmetic, so the honest claim is that the 27 can express and compute mathematics and prove within a declared set of axioms, never that they settle every truth. Geometry has a known axiom base too: Tarski's, built on betweenness and congruence, which map naturally onto CON along a line and EVA of two figures, and which could be checked axiom by axiom under the declared readings. Calculus then needs the fundamental theorem as a test: the slope of the integral of a function gives the function back, within the baseline, along every refinement path tried.

Every other way of evaluating, converted. Proposal (the user's). The universal integration needs more than mathematics in principle. Every ordinary way people already write an evaluation has to convert into the 27 and back: SQL conditions, joins, aggregates, window functions and CASE; spreadsheet formulas (SUM, IF, lookups, ranges); dataframe operations; MongoDB aggregation pipelines; Airtable and Notion formulas; Datalog rules; regular expressions; date and text functions. Most of these reduce to the same few needs: arithmetic and comparison (the number tower above), conditionals (an EVA that a SEG cuts by), lookups (CON), aggregates (SYN and SYN+), windows (REC), and readings that are not arithmetic at all, such as case folding, a calendar or a tokenizer, which become declared readings at Ground, pinned by content hash. Each function of each source language becomes a pattern written once in the 27 and stored as an entry (INS+), with a fast shortcut beside it that must give the same answer. The test for each source language is mechanical: list its functions (SQLite's core list, a spreadsheet's few hundred functions, the aggregation stages of a document store), translate each or name it as a gap, then run real expressions both ways, in the native engine and through the 27, on real data, and count disagreements. The earlier, larger EOQL translated 206 of 228 SQL queries, so the scale of the job is known, and that language had a function library this one forbids.

Result and Proposal, what SIG turned out to be (the full record, with every number and its source, is ledgers/pharos-eoql/SIG-ANSWER.md). The sessions then measured it on real reads from their own transcripts. Re-use fades as a power law, not an exponential, and plain recency carries most of the value. Frequency is the worst rule.
